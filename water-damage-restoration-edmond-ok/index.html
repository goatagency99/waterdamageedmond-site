Our water damage restoration process

01

Stop the source and make the area safe

We help you prioritize shutting off the water and addressing immediate electrical or slip hazards. If the source is ongoing, drying efforts will not hold.

Water mitigation vs. restoration

Water mitigation is the part that stops the damage from spreading: extraction, removing materials that cannot be dried, setting air movers and dehumidifiers, and checking readings every day. Restoration is what comes after, such as new drywall, flooring, paint, and trim. Insurance carriers usually expect mitigation to start quickly, so documenting the loss with photos on day one matters as much as the drying itself.

Homeowner questions

Water damage restoration FAQs

How fast should I start water damage restoration in Edmond, OK?+

Start the same day whenever possible because moisture spreads and humidity rises quickly inside walls and floors. Even after visible water is gone, wet drywall, insulation, and subflooring can keep releasing moisture into the air, which increases the chance of mold growth and material warping.

What should I do first while waiting for a restoration team?+

Stop the water source if it is safe, shut off power to affected areas if outlets or fixtures were wet, and move valuables and electronics to a dry space. If you can do it safely, blot or extract surface water and increase airflow, but avoid running HVAC if you suspect contaminated water or if water entered ductwork.

How do you tell how far the water traveled?+

We use moisture mapping with professional meters and inspection of likely pathways such as baseboards, drywall seams, flooring transitions, and cabinet bases. Water often travels under flooring and into wall cavities, so the wettest spot is not always where the leak began.

Do you need to remove drywall and flooring for water damage restoration?+

Sometimes, yes. If drywall is swollen, insulation is saturated, or water is trapped in a wall cavity, controlled removal is often the safest way to dry the structure fully. The goal is to remove only what is necessary to access wet areas and prevent long-term issues.

How long does structural drying usually take?+

Most drying jobs take about 3 to 5 days, but the timeline depends on materials, how long the water sat, and the amount of airflow that can be set up. Hardwood, plaster, and dense assemblies can take longer, and we confirm progress with moisture readings rather than the calendar.

Can you help with insurance documentation for water damage claims?+

Yes. We document affected areas, drying equipment, and moisture readings with notes and photos so the claim has a clear record of what happened and what work was required. Coverage decisions are made by the carrier, but good documentation reduces back and forth.

Do you handle sewage backups and contaminated water?+

Yes. Category 3 water requires additional safety controls, removal of porous materials that cannot be sanitized, and thorough cleaning and disinfection. If the loss involves sewage, we will explain what must be removed versus what can be cleaned safely.
